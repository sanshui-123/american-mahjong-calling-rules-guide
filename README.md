## 1. Can You Call a Discarded Tile for a Pair or a Single?

| Situation | Allowed? | Exact NMJL Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calling a discard to expose a **Single** mid-game | **No** | Never allowed for an intermediate exposure. |
| Calling a discard to expose a **Pair** mid-game | **No** | Pairs can never be exposed mid-game. |
| Calling a discard for a **Single or Pair that completes Mahjong** | **Yes** | Legal *only* when that exact tile is your 14th winning tile. |
| Calling a discard on a **Concealed (`C`) hand** mid-game | **No** | A concealed hand must stay hidden in your rack until Mahjong. |
| Calling a discard on a **Concealed (`C`) hand** for the winning tile | **Yes** | Legal *only* for the final tile that declares Mahjong. |
| Calling a discard for a **Pung (3), Kong (4), Quint (5), or Sextet (6)** on an Exposed (`X`) hand | **Yes** | Legal at any time during play; expose the full group on top of your rack. |

## 2. Joker Rules at a Glance

- **Where Jokers are legal:** Any group of **3 or more identical tiles** (Pungs, Kongs, Quints, Sextets).
- **Where Jokers are NEVER legal:** Singles, Pairs, or news/year/consecutive single-tile groupings like `2026` or `N E W S`.
- **Joker exchange timing:** Only on **your turn** (after drawing or calling, before discarding). You may swap a matching natural tile for a Joker in *any* player's exposed group—including your own.
